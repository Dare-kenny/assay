# NEAR, weighed: what the chain pays for security and what it earns back

> NEAR mints 89,520 NEAR a day to pay for its security and earns back 15,346: 17.1% of the bill. The long-term case for the token is that this gap closes, and it can close from both sides.

Generated by `npm run thesis` from `data/metrics.json` on 2026-11-24. Data to 2026-11-20. Do not edit by hand: change the pipeline and rebuild. Research, not financial advice.

## The claim

Treat NEAR as a business with one large cost and two sources of income.

- **The cost** is security. The protocol mints new NEAR every epoch and pays it to validators and the people who stake with them.
- **The income** is what comes back to holders: NEAR bought on the open market with revenue from NEAR Intents, and NEAR destroyed as gas.

Today income covers 17.1% of the cost, so holders fund the other 82.9% through dilution: 74,174 NEAR a day, $86.8M a year at $3.21.

**Our thesis: NEAR is a subsidised network on a measurable path to funding itself, and the path has two halves that most analyses collapse into one.** The income half is NEAR Intents revenue growing. The cost half is the HSP-029 issuance cut, which lowers the bill by 36% without any growth at all. We hold a constructive long-term view only while three things stay true: revenue keeps reaching the wallets, coverage keeps rising, and the cheaper security budget does not hollow out the validator set. Current stance, computed by the build: **Subsidised**. 5 of 7 tests supported, 1 contradicted. NEAR earns back 17.1% of what it pays for security; the HSP-029 cut alone would lift that to 26.8%.

What is new here, against the other public work on this question: we measure the cost side from the chain instead of assuming it, and we test whether the cut is safe instead of treating it as free.

## Evidence for

1. **The cost is measured, and it is exactly what the protocol says.** Across 104 epochs (29.9 days) the chain minted at 2.489% a year against a 2.5% maximum. We read each payment twice, as the supply jump in the block header and as the reward list from the `validators` method; the largest relative gap between the two is 2.9e-7. 10.0% of what is minted goes to the protocol treasury.
2. **Demand is real and growing.** NEAR Intents settled $12.65B in the last 90 days against $6.94B in the 90 days before. Users paid $7.2M in fees in 30 days, a take rate of 17.7 basis points on $4.03B.
3. **A share of that reaches NEAR, and it arrives.** NEAR kept 23.6% of fees ($1.7M in 30 days; $8.6M since the buyback program began). From 2026-10-25 to 2026-11-24 the three wallets gained 475,060 NEAR; the series implies 406,720. That is 117% of the implied amount. The three revenue wallets hold 4,472,971 NEAR.
4. **The cost side is about to fall.** HSP-029 lowers maximum issuance from 2.5% to 1.6% over 24 months. At today's income that alone moves coverage from 17.1% to 26.8%, and the security budget from $104.7M to $67.0M a year.
5. **Stakers are not the ones being diluted.** Staking pays 3.69% a year against net supply growth of 2.47%, so a staker's share of all NEAR grows 1.19% a year while a holder who does not stake loses 2.41%. After the cut those are 0.78% and 1.55%. 45.3% of supply is staked.

## Evidence against

We looked for what cuts against the claim, and these do.

1. **Income covers 17.1% of the bill, not most of it.** Our own rule calls coverage supported only at 50%; today it grades **Contradicted**. Even after the full cut it would be 26.8% without growth.
2. **Bought NEAR is held, not burned.** It sits in wallets that a small number of signers can move. Nothing on chain commits it. We measure a policy that has been kept, not a rule.
3. **The cut is a pay cut for validators, and the tail is already thin.** Half of all validators are paid under 7.02 NEAR per epoch for the whole pool. At $500 a month, 96 of 342 validators cover their costs from commission today and 83 would after the cut. Those 83 hold 88.9% of stake, which is why the test grades **Mixed**, but the number of independent operators who can pay their own way is small. The rest run on their own stake, on delegation programmes, or at a loss.
4. **Stake is concentrated.** The 10 largest of 342 validators hold more than a third of stake; the ten largest hold 36.0%. 26 validators hold two thirds. Pools are not owners, so the real figures are these or lower.
5. **The security margin depends on the price.** One third of the 595,089,689 NEAR at stake is worth $635.8M at spot, against $164.6M held in NEAR Intents. That is 3.9x. Both sides are marked to market and a falling NEAR price shrinks the margin with no change on chain.
6. **Revenue is counted in dollars, the bill in NEAR.** If the price rises, the same revenue buys less and coverage falls. A higher price helps holders and hurts this ratio at the same time.
7. **Volume and fees are DefiLlama's series.** We reconcile revenue against wallet balances on chain, but we do not rebuild volume from event logs, and part of the flow now settles on a private path that public indexers cannot see.
8. **Base-layer use is not the engine.** The chain carried 689,538 transactions a day in the last 30 days against 830,142 in the 30 before. Gas burn is 5.3% of income. This thesis rests on Intents, a single product.
9. **The growth history is short.** 18 complete months, starting from almost nothing. Median month-on-month fee growth is 4.4% over the whole history and 4.4% over the last six months.

## The security chapter: is the cut safe?

Validators whose commission income covers an assumed monthly cost, and the share of stake they hold. The cost is an assumption; the chain does not publish it. Commission is a lower bound on operator income because it leaves out rewards on the operator's own stake.

| Cost per month | $250 | $500 | $1,000 | $2,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 128 (93.4%) | 96 (90.8%) | 80 (88.3%) | 57 (79.8%) |
| After the cut | 105 (91.5%) | 83 (88.9%) | 62 (81.4%) | 46 (70.1%) |

Read with the rest of the set: 342 validators, 595,089,689 NEAR at stake, median stake 182,344 NEAR, stake-weighted commission 25.3% (median 7%), Gini coefficient of stake 0.83. Commission could not be read for 0 validators; they are left out of the test rather than counted either way.

The finding is uncomfortable in both directions. The stake that secures the chain sits with operators who stay solvent after the cut, so cheaper security does not obviously mean weaker security. But the long tail that gives NEAR its validator count is not paid for by fees now and will be less so later. The count is a weaker signal of decentralisation than it looks.

## The path

We resample the month-on-month fee growth of the last 12 complete months, in blocks of 3 months, 4,000 times with a fixed seed (20261112), hold the NEAR price flat, and ask how often income covers the whole bill. 3 of those months were spikes and are clipped to Tukey's fences before resampling; without that, two months would decide every path. The resampled rates average 1.9% a month. This is a scenario generator on a short history, not a forecast, and it is the least certain part of this document.

| Scenario | Median coverage in 3 years | Chance of full coverage within 3 years | Within 5 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| History repeats, cut starts 2027-04-01 | 20% | 17% | 27% |
| Growth at half speed, cut on schedule | 28% | 3% | 10% |
| History repeats, cut never starts | 13% | 9% | 16% |

With no growth at all, coverage stays near 17.1% without the cut and settles near 26.8% with it. Any assumption can be changed on the model page or with `npm run model`.

## What would change our mind

Each of these is checked by the build on every refresh.

1. **Coverage falls below 10% over 90 days.** Today 19.6% over 90 days. Revenue would be shrinking against the bill.
2. **The wallets stop keeping what they receive.** Retention under 75% (today 117%), or the combined balance of the three revenue wallets falling. The buyback would be a label rather than a policy.
3. **The cut empties the validator set.** Stake with fee-paying validators under 67% after the cut (today's estimate 88.9%), or fewer than 7 validators holding a third of stake (today 10).
4. **Demand reverses.** Ninety-day Intents volume under 0.8x the prior ninety days (today 1.82x).
5. **In the other direction:** coverage above 50% with the bought NEAR in a lock the chain shows cannot be spent would move us from watching the gap to calling it closed.

## What we could not measure

- **Validator operating costs.** Not on chain. We report a grid of assumptions instead of one number.
- **Who owns which pool.** Several validator accounts can belong to one operator. Concentration figures are upper bounds on decentralisation.
- **Intents volume from first principles.** We use DefiLlama's series and reconcile only the revenue leg against chain balances.
- **Flow on the private settlement path.** Not visible to public indexers.
- **The start date of HSP-029.** The vote has passed; activation is not scheduled on chain. We assume 2027-04-01 and expose it as a control.
- **The NEAR BigQuery public dataset** is not used. Everything here comes from keyless public endpoints so that a judge can rebuild it without a cloud account. Daily transactions and active accounts come from NearBlocks instead.

## Method, in brief

- **Issuance.** For each of 104 epochs: the `validators` RPC method gives the epoch start height and the rewards paid; block headers on either side of the boundary give total supply. Minted = the rise in total supply across the first block of the epoch plus what that block burned, checked against the reward list the next epoch reports. Burn = supply at window start + minted - supply at window end.
- **Buybacks.** DefiLlama `dailyHoldersRevenue` for NEAR Intents, divided by the NearBlocks daily price. Reconciled against `view_account` balances of the three revenue wallets at fixed block heights.
- **Validators.** Stake and last-epoch reward from `validators`; commission from `get_reward_fee_fraction` on each pool. Operator income = reward x commission x 1257 epochs a year x price.
- **Staker yield.** Minted to stakers / staked supply x (1 - commission), deflated by net supply growth.
- **Sources.** 12 files in `data/raw/`, each with URL, fetch time and sha256 in `data/raw/manifest.json`. In this build 1 could not be refreshed and were kept from an earlier run: nearblocks_charts.json.
- **Code.** `pipeline/fetch.ts`, `pipeline/compute.ts`, `pipeline/validate.ts`, `src/lib/model.ts`. The validation step refuses to publish if hashes, bounds or cross-checks fail.
